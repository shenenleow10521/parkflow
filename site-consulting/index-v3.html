A reservation service for one parking zone

Any shopper can reserve a dedicated zone (one level, ~200 bays); members’ fee is credited to their parking bill.

How ParkFlow works

  1. 01
    Reserve access

    Book a space in the zone before arrival. Members use their mall membership; the fee is credited to the bill.

  2. 02
    Verify at entry

    The existing LPR recognises the plate, confirms the booking and admits the car.

  3. 03
    Park in any available bay

    Choose any free bay in the zone. Unclaimed bookings are released after a 15-minute grace period.

Intelligent capacity management

A booking is a claim on the zone, not a fixed bay, so walk-ins keep their space. How the capacity is planned is shown later in this deck.

For shoppers

A more certain, time-saving parking experience.

For SDP

Booking income from non-members, member engagement and tenant sales to test in the pilot.

For the portfolio

Prove the model at one site, then replicate across SDP malls.

Proposed service. A whole level of about 200 bays is the planning size (Team Hexa site visit; level not named). Offline simulations were rerun at 200 bays (appendix). LPR and CCTV are site observations, to be confirmed with the mall. Benefits are pilot hypotheses, not measured results.

How the planner decides

Booking request

Accept if, at every 15-minute check during the booking's stay window, expected occupancy plus this booking stays within capacity.

  • Expected occupancy = parked cars still expected to be there + confirmed bookings, each counted as certain to arrive.
  • Safety margin = z × the spread of that estimate. z is set separately for bookings and walk-ins.
  • An optional cap on bookings per hour limits commitments in crowded windows.

Walk-in arrives

Admit if a bay is physically free and the same check holds at every 15-minute point inside the lookahead, using that car's expected stay.

  • Later walk-ins face the same check, so protecting bookings needs no separate demand forecast.
  • The lookahead starts at 3 hours; the pilot tests longer ones (5 to 8 hours) against observed stay lengths.

Example: a 7 PM booking made at 9 AM

9:00 accept only if capacity for 7 PM can be committed. 12:00 admit walk-ins unless a long stay could still be parked at 7 PM. 16:00 tighten as long stays begin to overlap. 18:00 confirm free bays. 19:00 honour the booking.

Stay model (layer 2)

  • Inputs: time of entry, how long the car has been parked, booking type and declared duration; plate history where available.
  • Output: the chance each parked car is still there at each check. It starts as one pooled stay distribution; gradient boosting is added only if pilot data show it helps.
  • Refresh: every 5 to 15 minutes from the plate ledger.

Safeguards

  • Verified counts decide admission; the plan only adds restrictions.
  • Uncertain counts pause new commitments (see fault handling).
  • The simple buffer rule on the next slide runs alongside in shadow mode as the comparison.

Settings the pilot calibrates

Lookahead, the two margins, the booking cap, and the planned show-up rate (1.0 means no overbooking).

Design specification. The rule and settings come from the simulation study in this appendix; none is calibrated to KLGCC data. Stay-model inputs other than entry time and age are proposed.

Baseline rule: a buffer that follows demand

The rule, refreshed every 15 minutes

B = clamp( requests per hour × hold time , floor , cap )

  • Requests per hour: booking requests in the last 30 minutes, doubled and smoothed.
  • Hold time: about 45 minutes, how long a held bay waits to be claimed.
  • Floor and cap: 2 bays, and 10% of the zone (10 bays at 100, 20 at 200).
  • Free bays = total − occupied − accepted claims − safety bays.

What a forecast changes

Only the input. It replaces last-30-minute requests with expected requests for the next hours, so the buffer can rise before a surge. In the offline study this did not beat reacting to recent traffic, so the pilot tests it in shadow mode against this rule.

Measured in the pilot

Booking rejection rate, idle bay-hours (free bays while a walk-in is turned away), and walk-ins admitted. The floor, cap and hold time are calibrated on the baseline counts.

How the commercial and value numbers are derived

Commercial model (slide 10)

  • Net factor 0.735 per RM of fee: 0.8 ÷ 1.06 (the operator takes 20% of the ex-tax fee) − 1.944% (card fee on the amount collected).
  • Service tax 6%: parking is taxed at 6% (Customs guide). A reservation fee is not listed as exempt, so the fee is treated as tax-inclusive. If untaxed, contribution is about 6% higher.
  • Gross per booking: RM3.04 at 50% members, from the 200-bay simulation (40 synthetic days per volume; deposits credited to members’ bills).
  • Displaced parking revenue: cars sent to other levels pay the same KLGCC tariff, so only walk-ins who leave the mall are a loss. The pilot measures it.
  • Break-even at 50% members: running cost at 14 bookings a day, 5-year recovery at 32, 3-year at 43.

Value to SDP (slide 12)

  • Gap: RM70.6k ÷ 5 − RM6.4k contribution at ≈22 bookings a day = RM7.7k a year.
  • Sales needed: gap ÷ rent clause (5–15%, Tropicana valuation) = RM51k–154k. Turnover rent has a floor: extra sales below it add zero.
