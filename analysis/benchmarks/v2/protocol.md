# Frozen offline benchmark protocol, 8 November 2026

This is a new research harness, not the deployed browser application or a KL East field trial. Its first run uses the settings below without selecting weights, scenarios or seeds to favour the optimizer. All booking-demand and trip data here are explicitly synthetic. Historical parameters are inherited from the earlier simulator as scenario inputs, not newly validated local facts.

- Occupancy: original public UCI Parking Birmingham CSV. A reading becomes available at the **next** half-hour boundary. Predict 30/60/120/180 minutes. Six outer weekly tests use disjoint earlier training, blend and interval-calibration weeks. Compare persistence, last week, adjusted last week, LightGBM and ensemble. Do not equate these new samples with the earlier rounded-time results.
- Synthetic data: seeds 10000–10159 for training days; 20000–20039 for calibration; 30000 onward for 60 paired test days. Every policy receives the same immutable external requests, arrivals, stays, show/no-show and late-arrival events. Future outcomes are hidden from ordinary policy inputs.
- Time: 10:00–23:00, decisions every 30 minutes, six planning buckets including the immediate action (current through +150 minutes; each bucket spans 30 minutes, a 180-minute window). Requests are made 30 minutes before their due bucket. Grace lasts one additional bucket. Observe departures, handle arrivals and expiries, accept requests, then admit walk-ins. Resolve remaining accepted arrivals after closing without accepting new requests.
- Demand: inherited two-peak arrival shape; mean 60 member requests/day before day-level variation. Day intensity is lognormal (sigma 0.30) and affects both streams. Walk-in stays are lognormal with median 87 minutes and sigma log(155/44)/(2×0.6745). Members request 60–240 minutes and actual stays have lognormal noise. These inputs and batch arrivals need local calibration.
- Safety: every accepted booking holds one **currently free physical bay** until it arrives or its grace expires. Every ordinary admission preserves all outstanding claims and two spare bays. Predictions never authorize a promise. All policies share this guard. This is deliberately conservative with long lead times. A batch-perfect, independently verified ledger is assumed; undetected sensor faults are outside its safety guarantee.
- Policy comparisons: fixed extra buffer 5; fixed extra buffer 10; reactive next-bucket forecast buffer; 180-minute LP with historical averages; identical LP with learned forecasts; oracle-input rolling LP reference. No common 20-booking quota hides acceptance differences. Only the immediately executable walk-in quota is applied; future LP acceptance values are plans, not commitments.
- LP objective: 5 × planned member acceptances + 1 × walk-ins. Capacity constraints use expected occupancy, while the current action also obeys the hard guard. Future cohorts use training-derived survival kernels. No-show uncertainty is not used to release real promises. Test objective weights 1 and 10 separately; these are operating preferences, not economics.
- Ablation: the optimizer and objective remain constant; historical slot/weekday demand plus memoryless mean-duration survival versus LightGBM demand plus learned age-conditioned survival. This is a **combined forecast bundle** ablation, not isolation of each individual input. Oracle inputs know future offered counts and remaining durations of current vehicles, but retain fitted future-cohort kernels. The reference is **not** a globally optimal upper bound.
- Forecasts: report synthetic cumulative request-count MAE/WAPE and calibrated 80% interval coverage at 30/60/120/180 minutes. Score departure-count MAE at 15/30 minutes on fixed-5-policy snapshots. All forecast training precedes calibration and testing; booking/turnover results cannot establish local predictive validity.
- Sizing: 50/100/150 bays with identical external demand; also 50/150 with demand scaled to capacity. Never select capacity solely from total admissions without checking rejection and utilization.
- Stress: demand spike; stays ×1.6; increased late arrivals; increased no-shows; biased forecasts (demand ×0.5 and predicted departures ×1.5); detected count drift; network outage. Detected faults pause **new** commitments and walk-ins; existing promises retain reserved bays and use a separate verified local ledger. This does not test undetected drift or failure of the local ledger.
- Outcomes per day: requests, accepted bookings, accepted arrivals served, capacity-denied accepted arrivals, expired late arrivals, no-shows, walk-ins, booking rejection, idle protected bay-hours **while walk-ins are rejected**, physical over-capacity, claim invariant violations, fault-paused requests and solver failures/latency. Idle bay-hours use physically free bays at the bucket start × 0.5 hours, including pending-claim/margin bays; this is a discrete proxy, not a continuous-time integral. Expired/late requests and safety failures remain distinct. A no-show is not counted as a capacity failure.
- Inference: pair daily outcomes across policies; report bootstrap 95% intervals (2000 date-level resamples). Report every policy and failed solve. A synthetic CI measures Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on this generator; it does not capture real-world model uncertainty.
- No retention, willingness-to-pay, installed-cost or commercial-return validation is inferred from these runs.

Implementation references: [SciPy HiGHS linear programming](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.linprog.html), [LightGBM Python API](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Python-API.html), [UCI Parking Birmingham](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/482/parking+birmingham).

## Diagnostic additions after the initial run

The initial 2,880 policy-day run found identical base-case actions for the average, ML and oracle rolling planners. Before interpreting buffer gains as optimizer gains, two additional diagnostics were specified and the entire suite rerun with identical original seeds and weights:

1. **Fixed zero-extra-buffer baseline**: preserves the same two-bay safety margin and all accepted promises, with no discretionary spare buffer. This isolates whether the LP adds value over simply executing the guard. All seven policies are reported, including this diagnostic.
2. **Undetected-drift negative control**: eight occupied-car records disappear from both the count and independent ledger for four buckets. This deliberately breaks the verified-state assumption. The physical boundary refuses attempted over-capacity entry; existing promises can still fail. Report this scenario separately from detected-fault tests; do not conceal failures or use safe-suite zero counts to imply resilience to undetected errors.
